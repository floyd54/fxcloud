# v0.1.4 - folders that belong to a group

The third work line (`concepts/groups.md`): **"Gruppenordner" answered
as first-class storage principals**, not as shares routed through a
group.
A group is its own storage principal with managed membership, its own
quota and manager/member roles; a member bootstraps its top-level
folder, every member sees it in their own root with the group's badge,
and the whole tree is worked directly - no individual shares involved.

## What changed

- **Groups with roles and their own quota.** Administrators create,
  rename and delete groups in a new **Group folders** section of the
  user administration: name, quota in MiB against current consumption,
  and the member list with a role toggle (manager/member). The creator
  becomes the group's first manager; a group is deleted only once it is
  empty - no members, no folders (trashed rows count) - and the
  refusal is a 409, never a silent cascade.
- **Bootstrap with one button.** A member creates the group's
  top-level folder from the root listing (**New group folder...**,
  offered only while you belong to a group): `POST /api/files/folder`
  with `group` and no parent. The creator stays the row's `owner_id`
  (audit trail, blob namespace) while `owner_group_id` makes the group
  pay the quota - and attribution follows every move in and out of the
  group.
- **One tree for every member.** Members find the group's root in
  their own root (a badge next to the share badge), work the whole
  subtree - upload, rename, move, trash - and one name per parent is
  unique across *all* members, not per creator. A group folder cannot
  be shared out to an individual (403): membership is what grants
  access. Group trash is visible to every member, and any member may
  restore or purge it.
- **Quota per group.** Group usage = live sizes plus versions of the
  group's rows, trash included (a trashed row bills until the purge),
  checked on every write like a user's quota (507 when full). Members
  never spend their own budget on group writes.
- **Key distribution (E2EE).** The manager of a group's top-level
  folder opens its row menu (**Distribute folder keys**) and wraps the
  folder's key record to one member at a time - the same envelope
  shape and AAD as a share's, so the member's tab opens it through the
  same key map. The pending list shows every member without an
  envelope (the manager's own pair included) plus whether they have a
  public key yet; envelopes carry the same 8192-character cap as share
  envelopes, and removing a member deletes their envelopes for that
  group with them. Inside a group the record line holds: the first key
  record on a row may be set by any member, swapping an existing one
  is manager-only (403).
- **Eleven new endpoints** (7 admin + 4 member), documented in
  API.md's new *Group folders* section; the user guide gains a chapter
  (6. Group folders) and ENCRYPTION.md 5.5 records that a group key
  envelope is a share envelope by another name.

## Compatibility

Self-applying: one migration (`017_groups.sql`) adds the three tables
(`groups`, `group_members`, `group_keys`), the `owner_group_id` column
and its indexes, so restarting the new binary is the whole upgrade.
Existing rows keep `owner_group_id = NULL` (personal); no endpoint
changed shape, and a browser sitting on an open session needs no hard
reload. The server never sees a group key in readable form: it stores
opaque envelopes only.

## Tests

The suite grew by 41 checks to **663**: admin CRUD (creator = first
manager, case-insensitive duplicate 409, admin-only 403), the member's
own view, bootstrap attribution, the cross-member sibling 409, the root
badge and visibility (member sees it, non-member does not), a member's
upload landing in the shared subtree, the share-out 403, the
pending/distribute/upsert matrix with its manager gate and the
no-key-material answer for a member without an envelope, the record
line inside a group, the group's quota (507 + used-bytes), trash and
restore across members, personal <-> group move attribution both ways,
member removal cutting access and envelopes, the empty-group delete,
and the i18n keys in all three dictionaries. The unit tests grew by
four to **52** (group CRUD, membership and key rows, and the
manager-vs-member role mask).
